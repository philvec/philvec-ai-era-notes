## 2026-05-06

- I've been writing this on whiteboards across my employer's offices for months, inviting anyone to challenge it: AI will outperform human-led orgs at resource allocation — capital, logistics, people. That's a structural challenge to capitalism itself, not just automation. The way we work, make money, learn, use computers for anything — that all will dramatically change. It's the end of these things as we know it, and the change is almost here — it has already started. We are at the exact turning point, right now. Humans will still matter — I strongly believe and hope so — but how is genuinely open.

- Most people, even those following AI closely, seem to still think of it as a cool feature — impressive, imperfect, but fundamentally a tool. Not long ago I agreed with LeCun: LLMs are an inefficient midpoint, incapable of real reasoning or awareness, the real breakthrough is yet to come. The AGI question — real reasoning, abstract thinking, awareness — remains wide open and fascinating, and ARC-AGI keeps it honest. I remain devoted to the idea that the answer is NOT pure scaling; LeCun and the ARC-AGI authors are right to resist that. And yet — I'm starting to think the next meaningful leap may not be a technology breakthrough at all, but **practice**: the process of humans learning how to work with these systems, even as the technology stays the same. "AI agents" doesn't mean what it meant two years ago, or even one year ago when industries first productionised the term. The point of view has changed enormously.

- That evolution leads me to **AI workers** — and I mean something specific by that, distinct from agents. Workers are long-lived: persistent memory, developed skills, and a strong soul — genuine personality and character, not a single-goal system prompt with a toolset bolted on. They're closer to what we now call full assistants, but elevated: no longer sidekicks. Let them be the real knights — let *them* have assistants, spawn agents, delegate. Scale that up and you get a fleet of workers, each with their own domain and entourage, led by a single human. That's the org of the near future.
